Lab 9: Smart Factory Anomaly AI (Step-by-Step)

Objective: Use your smartphone's accelerometer to collect data, train a Machine Learning model in Edge Impulse, and deploy a live classifier to detect an anomaly: "Dangerous Machine Vibrations".

Step 1: Create an Edge Impulse Account
Open your laptop browser and go to studio.edgeimpulse.com.
Sign up for a free developer account.
Click + Create new project. Name it Factory Anomaly Detection.
When asked what type of data you are dealing with, select Accelerometer data.

Step 2: Connect your Smartphone as an IoT Sensor
We need to collect "Training Data" to teach the AI.
On the left menu, click Devices.
Click the Connect a new device button (top right).
Select Use your mobile phone. A QR code will appear on your laptop screen.
Take out your real smartphone, open the Camera app, and scan the QR code.
Click the link. Your phone will ask for permission to use its motion sensors. Click Allow.
Look at your laptop: Your phone will now appear in the Devices list with a green dot!

Step 3: Collect Training Data
We need to teach the AI what 3 different states feel like.
On your laptop, click Data acquisition (left menu).
On the right side, you will see the "Record new data" panel. Ensure your phone is selected.
Set the Sensor to Accelerometer and Frequency to 62.5Hz.
Collect State 1: IDLE
Change the Label to Idle. Set Length to 10000 ms (10 seconds).
Put your phone flat on the desk. Do not touch it.
Click Start sampling. Wait 10 seconds.
Collect State 2: NORMAL
Change the Label to Normal.
Pick up your phone and gently move it back and forth (like a smoothly running machine).
Click Start sampling. Wait 10 seconds.
Collect State 3: ANOMALY
Change the Label to Anomaly.
Shake your phone violently and randomly (simulating a machine breaking down).
Click Start sampling. Wait 10 seconds.
Repeat this process until you have about 1 minute of data for each of the 3 labels.

Step 4: Design & Train the AI (The Neural Network)
On the left menu, click Impulse design -> Create impulse.
An "Impulse" is the AI pipeline.
Click Add a processing block -> Choose Spectral Analysis (Great for repeating vibrations).
Click Add a learning block -> Choose Classification (Keras).
Click Save Impulse.
On the left menu, click Spectral features. Click Save parameters, then click Generate features. (You will see a cool 3D map of your data!).
On the left menu, click Classifier.
Click the Start training button.
Wait 1-2 minutes while the Cloud trains your Neural Network!
When finished, look at the Model testing performance (aim for >85% accuracy).

Step 5: Test the AI Live!
On the left menu, click Deployment (or go back to your smartphone screen).
On your smartphone browser (which should still be open), click Switch to Classification Mode at the bottom of the screen.
The AI model will download directly into your phone's web browser!
The Test:
Put the phone on the desk. The screen will read Idle: 0.99.
Gently move it. The screen will read Normal: 0.95.
Shake it violently. The screen will instantly spike to Anomaly: 0.99!

💡 Instructor Note: Why this lab is perfect for Week 10
If a student asks, "How does this connect to our ESP32?" you can explain:
"In a physical project, Step 5 is different. Instead of testing on the phone, you click 'Export as C++ Library' in Edge Impulse. It gives you a .zip file containing the trained Neural Network. You include that file in your ESP32 code (using VS Code/Arduino), and the ESP32 can run the AI offline forever!"
Because they are doing a Wokwi simulation for their final project, they cannot deploy C++ libraries to Wokwi easily, which is why testing it on the smartphone browser perfectly completes the learning objective without breaking the simulator!
